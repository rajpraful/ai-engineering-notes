The Great Barrier Reef is the world's largest coral reef system, stretching over 2,300 kilometres along the coast of Queensland, Australia. It is made up of nearly 3,000 individual reefs and around 900 islands. The reef is home to thousands of species of fish, molluscs, sea turtles, dolphins and sharks, and it can even be seen from space.

Scientists warn that rising ocean temperatures are causing mass coral bleaching. When the water gets too warm, corals expel the tiny algae living in their tissues, turn completely white and, if conditions do not improve, eventually die. Protecting the reef has become one of the most important conservation challenges of our time.
